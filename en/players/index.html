Part two of the slot market research: why specific hits are popular and what players love about them. Sources: slot reviews, academic papers, UK regulator data, surveys and forums.

01 — Key takeaway

A growing multiplier keeps players, not the theme

The core of almost every hit is visible progress inside the bonus: the win multiplier builds up, doubles or “sticks” to a cell and doesn’t reset until the round ends

Layer 1

One clear dream

The top win fits in one phrase and makes a clip: “a screen of Rich Wildes” (Book of Dead), “a x1000 orb” (Gates of Olympus).

Layer 2

A recognisable character

Moves from sequel to sequel and sells the next game: Zeus, the lollipop, the fisherman, Smokey the raccoon, the Pirots parrots, the tiger.

Layer 3

Value in a rare bonus

The bonus lands roughly once every 400–450 spins, and a paid bonus buy shortens the way. The same math breeds both delight and complaints.

Summary takeaway from ~25 slots analysed, research and player voice

Players’ love and hate are two sides of the same math. Orbs that “didn’t drop”, a 100-bet bonus that returned 15, and hundreds of dead spins come from the same mechanism as the viral max win. “Four special symbols” refers to the Super Scatter in Gates of Olympus Super Scatter: four of them at bonus trigger pay 50,000x instantly.

02 — Glossary: slot math

How to read the numbers here

“x” after a number

Times the bet: “5,000x” is a win of 5,000 bets; a “100x bonus” costs 100 bets

RTP

Share of bets the slot returns to players over time: 96% means 96 of every 100 bets come back on average

Hit rate

Share of spins with any win at all. 50% means a win on every second spin

Volatility

How wins are spread at the same RTP: high means rare but big; low means frequent but small

Max win

The biggest possible win, the slot’s “ceiling”, usually in bets: 5,000x, 50,000x

Bonus, free spins

Free spins with special rules, triggered by scatters. This is where most of a hit’s value lies

Bonus buy

Enter the bonus right away for a fixed price (e.g. 100 bets) instead of waiting

Stake data

Number of real bets per game at online casino Stake per quarter (NEXT.io Spin Charts)

Plain-language definitions for this deck

03 — Glossary: mechanics

Terms you’ll see later on

Cascade (tumble)

Winning symbols vanish and new ones drop in, so one spin can win several times

No paylines

8+ matching symbols anywhere pay (scatter-pays), or a group of adjacent ones (clusters)

Wild, sticky wild

A wild substitutes any symbol. A sticky wild stays in place until the bonus ends

Multiplier

Multiplies the win: x10 is 10 times more. In hits, the multiplier builds up during the bonus

Hold & win

Cash symbols lock and you get 3 respins; each new cash symbol resets them to 3

Megaways

Symbols per reel change every spin, up to 117,649 ways to win

Super Scatter

A special Pragmatic symbol: 1–4 of them at bonus trigger pay from 100x to 50,000x instantly

VS symbol

Hacksaw’s “duel” symbol: expands into a full wild reel with a x2 to x100 multiplier

Near-miss

An “almost win”: e.g. one scatter short of the bonus

Mechanics examples from the slot analysis in this report (OLBG, SlotCatalog, Bigwinboard)

Super Scatter in Gates of Olympus Super Scatter: 1–4 symbols at bonus trigger pay 100x / 500x / 5,000x / 50,000x. Megaways was popularised by Big Time Gaming in Bonanza Megaways.

05 — Sequels

Same slot, higher ceiling

Winning spins

50 → 43%

Sweet Bonanza → Sweet Bonanza 1000. Small wins land less often in the sequel

Wins of 1,000+ bets

3× more

Once per ~261k spins in the original → once per ~84k in the 1000 version. Same RTP: money moved from small wins to rare big ones

“1000” and Super Scatter sequels keep the art, sound, grid and ~96.5% RTP and change only the top end: Gates of Olympus orbs go from up to x500 → up to x1,000, Sugar Rush cells from up to x128 → up to x1,024, max win from 5,000 → 15,000–100,000 bets.

What it means: players don’t need to relearn anything, and the dream got bigger. The flip side is the “same slot with a new number” jab in almost every review.

Source: OLBG (calculated from specs, not real sessions), NEXT.io

Exact OLBG figures: hit rate of 50% for Sweet Bonanza and 42.92% for Sweet Bonanza 1000; a 1,000x+ win once per 261,097 spins vs once per 83,557. Gates of Olympus Super Scatter took more than 225m bets on Stake in its debut quarter. Reviewers call Super Scatter a standard mechanic under a new name.

09 — Fortune Tiger case

Popularity built partly on fake wins

67%

of casino game searches in Brazil in 2025 were for Fortune Tiger (Aposta Legal survey). The game is mentioned in 100+ Brazilian songs

Demo

Influencers got demo accounts with fake money, rigged to win, while followers were sent to the regular site (SBT News investigation)

Aug 2024

Since 1 Aug 2024, Brazil has banned ads that present betting as a path to wealth; criminal cases have been opened against bloggers

What it means: this kind of popularity is a poor signal of player taste. But the mechanic itself is simple and strong: 3×3 for a vertical screen, one magic moment, a “cute” mascot.

Sources: SBT News, Yogonet Brasil, pt.wikipedia, Agência Lupa

Agência Lupa experts call the tiger’s “cuteness” a deliberate strategy: a familiar childlike character builds trust and masks risk. Media cited an actual RTP of around 20% on unlicensed platforms; the figure is unverified. Influencers were paid about R$100 per 10 depositing players.

10 — Crash games

A competitor that sells control

Control

The player decides when to cash out

Aviator: the multiplier grows while the plane flies; cash out before it flies away. Chicken Road: a chicken crosses the road step by step, with difficulty modes like levels. A slot can only offer waiting.

Social

Chat and others’ bets

In Aviator you see who bets and cashes out how much, plus chat and group bets. Slots have almost no social layer.

Demand

Chicken Road is #1 in search

The world’s most searched casino game in 2025 by the Blask search interest index. Aviator audience figures (10m+ players a month) are a Spribe claim, unverified.

Sources: NEXT.io / Blask Awards, iGamingBusiness, Stage and Cinema

Crash competes with slots not for revenue but for the first session and lobby space. Its strengths are a sense of control and social play, which slots barely offer. Chicken Road’s 98% RTP is also a developer claim.

12 — Theme

The best theme explains the mechanic

“Three Little Pigs”

Houses grow from straw to a mansion, and the wolf blows them down to reveal the win: Huff N’ Puff

Western

The duel, revolver and bullets explain duel symbols and “charges”: Wanted, Duel at Dawn, Tombstone

Fishing

The fisherman “catches” fish with cash drawn on them: Fishin’ Frenzy and Big Bass

Money reel

Digits on the reels add up to a sum: Money Coming reads without rules

Sources: CDC Gaming (Light & Wonder and Aristocrat interviews), Bigwinboard, SlotCatalog

A Light & Wonder designer explains choosing “Three Little Pigs”: the tale is light, cartoonish and recognisable, has no dark side, and the verbs in the title explain the mechanic by themselves. The opposite example: Pragmatic sold the same math to two audiences by swapping the theme, Gates of Olympus and Starlight Princess.

14 — Research: sound and celebration

Feel amplifies good math

Win sound

+24%

overestimate with sound

+15%

without sound

Players played two sessions on a slot simulator, with and without win sounds, then estimated how many times they won more than their bet. They overestimated in both sessions, but by 24% with sound and 15% without.

Experiment, University of Waterloo (Canada), 2013–14, 96 people

What it means: sound makes players remember more wins than there were. Most participants preferred to play with sound.

Loss disguised as a win

58%

with celebration

24%

without it

A payout below the bet comes with a celebration animation. Of four games, 58% chose the generous one (115% payback) with this celebration, and 24% chose it without. For the stingy one (85%): 15% and 3%.

Experiment, Graydon, Stange and Dixon, 2018, ~33 people

What it means: the trick only helps a game that already pays well. It doesn’t rescue bad math.

Sources: University of Waterloo (Dixon et al.); Graydon, Stange, Dixon — Journal of Gambling Studies

In English-language literature, a loss presented as a win is called an LDW (loss disguised as a win). In another study by Dixon and co-authors (2018, 136 people), about 76% chose a 20-line game over a single-line one at the same bet, and players reacted to such “wins” the same way as to a real small win. With sound, Waterloo participants also showed a higher skin conductance response (a sign of arousal).

15 — Research: bonus and rhythm

Events and peaks hold players, not the word “free”

Free spins

No effect

Participants chose between versions of a game. Free spins on their own didn’t make a game more attractive. People chose free spins with extra sound, animation and more frequent wins, and even the same “bonus” with no free spins at all.

Three experiments, Taylor, Macaskill and Hunt, 2017

What it means: players respond to the bonus event and its presentation, not to the free-spins mechanic as such.

Event rhythm

763k sessions

Researchers looked for what predicts how many spins a person makes in one session. The best predictor is how often something happens in the game. Second is the largest single win in the session.

Real data from a European operator: 43,731 players. Auer and Griffiths, 2023

What it means: frequent events and a vivid peak retain players better than theme and visuals.

Sources: Taylor, Macaskill, Hunt — Journal of Gambling Studies; Auer & Griffiths — Nottingham Trent University

Harris and Griffiths review (2018): fast games feel more exciting and are linked to longer play and difficulty stopping, especially among problem gamblers; the evidence is mostly correlational. Percy et al. (2021, 4,281 regular players at two UK operators): at the casino operator, playing less volatile slots was linked to smaller losses and shorter sessions.

16 — Research: near-miss and RTP

What’s unproven and what’s misunderstood

Near-miss

10 of 11

A review of 51 experiments from 1991–2015. In 10 of 11 studies that measured skin response, a near-miss aroused more than a regular loss. But in 2019, three new experiments failed to confirm that it makes people play longer.

Review by Barton et al., 2017; experiments by Pisklak, Yong, Spetch, 2019

What it means: the trick creates tension but isn’t proven to retain. And players dislike “teasers” that don’t pay.

Understanding RTP

32%

of players misread the standard RTP wording. When odds were shown as a likely loss in pounds, understanding rose and willingness to play fell by 7 percentage points.

Behavioural Insights Team experiment, 2022, 5,311 people

What it means: mass-market players don’t read RTP, while enthusiasts are very sensitive to it: Pirots’ 94% RTP and Hacksaw versions down to ~88% draw complaints.

Sources: Barton et al.; Pisklak et al. (University of Alberta); BIT via Gambling Industry News

An important ethical caveat: sound, celebration, speed, near-misses and losses disguised as wins are studied in academic literature as harm factors. For a product, this is a regulatory and reputational risk. In the same BIT experiment, a free-spins offer raised the share who chose to play from 70% to 75%, even among those who understood the odds. Newall and co-authors (2022, 2,433 people): the wording “the casino keeps 10%” and a volatility warning slightly reduce betting.

17 — UK regulator

Mechanics removed, players stayed

Since October 2021, UK online slots have banned autoplay, turbo mode, spins faster than 2.5 seconds, slam stops and celebrating losses as wins. The regulator compared play before and after:

What it means: players now play shorter but more often. A slot has to work without accelerators and without bonus buy, which has been effectively banned in the UK since 2020: the regulator found it encourages chasing losses.

Source: UKGC (UK Gambling Commission), evaluation of game design changes; statistics to March 2026

Bets above £2 fell from 3.9% to 3.3% of spins, and the share of accounts losing more than £1,000 a month from 1.9% to 1.7%. After the bonus buy ban, all six operators that offered it removed it. No peer-reviewed research on bonus buy was found at all.

18 — Player voice

They love tension, hate broken expectations

Love

Sticky wilds that stay on the grid and build tension

Multipliers that grow through cascades

Bonus retriggers that extend the round

Surprise features outside the bonus

Hacksaw bonus buys: “return more on average” (Stake forum)

Hate

Bought a bonus for 100 bets, got back less than 20

Fanfare for small change and a flat reaction to a big win

Coin counting and animations you can’t skip

“Teasers” and near-misses that pay nothing

Pragmatic has 1.1 out of 5 on Trustpilot from 1,202 reviews, yet the share of those same games keeps growing: the negativity reflects reach. Russian-speaking players almost unanimously suspect that after bonus buy was added, the natural bonus started landing less often; whether or not that’s technically true, the distrust is real. Streamers on Stake judge slots by buy return and ceiling; the casual audience by jackpots, graphics and pace.

19 — Takeaways for slot design

How to design a slot

One visible counter

The multiplier is a physical object with animation and sound, not a number in the corner; it builds up and doesn’t reset

Theme explains the rule

Look for a metaphor where the mechanic is obvious without a help screen

A franchise character

Reads on a square lobby icon and survives a change of setting, like Smokey or the Pirots parrots

Celebration to scale

Animations are skippable, sound grows with the bonus, the background loop is long. Celebrating losses is banned in the UK, so this is also a regulatory requirement

Honest anticipation

Teasers that don’t pay and progress that resets annoy more than having none at all

Lines by volatility

A calm line for the mass market (medium volatility, like the Le series or Big Bass) alongside hardcore flagships

Portrait, one hand

A small grid and a thumb spin: both UX and a clip format for social media

Bonus buy: keep the top bonus unbuyable and show the buy RTP; the slot must work without it too

The core brief for the design team: the player buys a story within a single round — trigger, build-up, payoff. Theme, character and sound are worth exactly as much as they make that story readable.

20 — Takeaways for the lobby

How to show slots in the lobby

Franchises as families

“Gates of Olympus: Original / 1000 / Super Scatter” with a max-win badge: 5,000x / 15,000x / 50,000x

Volatility and the gist

A volatility filter and a one-line mechanic: “the fisherman collects fish”, “multipliers add up”

Your version’s RTP

Hacksaw gives casinos versions with RTP down to ~88%, Pirots has 94%: the top complaint of enthusiasts. Open RTP on the tile is a trust argument

Classics for newcomers

Starburst, Book of Dead, Big Bass make a better first experience than extreme Nolimit

Crash and social layer

A separate crash shelf, “playing now”, a win feed and tournaments for “status seekers” (21% in the 2019 survey)

No fake wins

The Brazil case: influencer demo accounts quickly turn into criminal cases and ad bans

On Stake, sequels outperform originals, but operators keep originals in the main lobby out of inertia

Players use two value frames: entertainment per unit of time and the win ceiling. A lobby that helps choose by these frames matches how people actually pick slots. “Out of inertia” is a Blask observation: the original Gates of Olympus sits in lobbies as “default inventory” even though players have moved to the 1000 version.

Takeaway

The player buys a story within one round

Trigger, build-up, payoff. Feel amplifies good math but doesn’t rescue bad math.

Differentiator
