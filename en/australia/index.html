The Interactive Gambling Act bans offering online casinos to Australians. The credit card and crypto ban (June 2024) applies only to licensed wagering operators — offshore casinos still accept both.

For a licensed product, Australia is a map of player tastes, not a market to enter.

Sources: H2GC, AGBrief, Blask, Dept of Infrastructure

Steady blocking alongside a stable brand count points to fast rotation of mirrors and clones — an inference from comparing the figures, not a measurement. The share of crypto deposits by Australians is not published anywhere, and there is no Stake data for Australia.

06 — Where the taste comes from

Online sells the club feel without club brands

Overlap with land-based casinos

Mechanic

Hold & Spin made Aristocrat’s Lightning Link and Dragon Link the top-earning machines in clubs. Online leaders replicate it and the Mini / Minor / Major / Grand ladder.

Themes

Egypt and Asian luck echo club classics such as Queen of the Nile and Dragon Link’s dragon themes.

Club scale

~15,800 Dragon Link machines in NSW and Queensland alone (May 2025). The real-money originals are not available to Australians online.

Not proven by surveys: 3 Oaks and Playson lead because they fill the online gap for a format players know from clubs. No studio names club pokies as a reference.

Sources: CDC Gaming (J.P. Morgan), PokiesAustralia (affiliate), SlotCatalog, OLBG

Online, the mechanic itself is not new: reviewers describe Pragmatic’s Wolf Gold (2017), with its Money Respin on three resetting respins, as widely copied. In 2022, 17.4% of regular club players also played online pokies.

14 — Visual language

Studios deliberately keep it familiar

Playson, Coin Strike 2

Kept the original’s blue background, fruit, lighting and rock soundtrack. Added gold lightning, a new Super Strike animation and layered audio; the grid grew from 3×3 to 3×4. Sequels sometimes outperform the originals — no figures given.

3 Oaks

Themes are chosen for familiarity; differentiation comes from modifiers: Extender adds three rows during respins — a visible change to the grid.

The standard vocabulary of a hit: gold value coins, lightning and fire, an object beside the grid (volcano, pot), a named jackpot ladder

Overlap with land-based casinos

This vocabulary reads as a stylisation of club Dragon Link and Lightning Link; studios do not confirm deliberate imitation.

Sources: iGaming Business (Playson sponsored content), SiGMA, Casinos.com

Yggdrasil’s CEO says reskins lose commercial value and distribution algorithms penalise sameness. Yet the Australian market rewards familiarity itself — the winner is whoever executes the standard most cleanly.

16 — Characters

Two ways to build a franchise

Mascot

Elvis Frog, Zeus

BGaming: “art attracts players, maths keeps them”. Elvis was designed as a bright, instantly recognisable character. Zeus triggers multiplier orbs.

Object

3 Oaks and Playson coins

The brand rests on the coin, volcano, pot and lightning. The princess in Sunlight Princess is just a wild; the press release doesn’t mention her.

Risk

Characters in ads

In NZ, fictional characters are banned in advertising; in the UK, banners with cartoon characters were ruled appealing to children (2025).

Overlap with land-based casinos

In Australian venues, features with comic characters hooked problem gamblers more strongly (2014). Aristocrat’s club titles Miss Kitty and Mo’ Mummy are in the casinos.com 2026 hall of fame.

Sources: European Gaming (BGaming), Gambling.com NZ, CMS / ASA, Schottler 2014, casinos.com

The 3 Oaks coin strategy, where the brand rests on an object rather than a character, is safer from a regulatory standpoint in New Zealand. In-game themes are not restricted — the risk lies in using characters in marketing.

17 — Takeaways for online slots

Building an online slot for AU/NZ

Hold & Win as the base

A high-value coin is the key asset; the counter reset is the key event

Ladder above the grid

Mini / Minor / Major / Grand always visible; coin values are the highest-contrast elements

Familiar costume, cleaner

Egypt, Asian luck, fruit; stand out through coin animation and symbol clarity

Celebrate by size

No celebration at or below stake returned; small wins quietly, big wins loudly

No autoplay or turbo

The game must hold attention at a manual pace; bonus buy configurable for NZ

Character inside the mechanic

The character triggers or explains the mechanic rather than fronting the ads

Sequels on the Playson model: keep the look, amplify one effect, grow the grid slightly

Visible modifiers such as 3 Oaks’ Extender offer a reason for new animation that is rare in this genre. The key Hold & Win animations are exactly what reviewers don’t examine — this is room for differentiation.

18 — Takeaways for the lobby

Building an online lobby for AU/NZ

“Hold & Win / Link” row

Grouped by family: Coin Volcano, Dragon Pearls, Coin Strike, Sun of Egypt — players seek out the format

Second showcase

Global high-volatility hits (Gates of Olympus, Sweet Bonanza) shown separately

Jackpots inside the lobby

They can’t be advertised in NZ — a Grand badge on the tile and a pool banner become the main stage

Responsible gambling as UI

Session timer in the header, pause screen with countdown, limits in onboarding, one game at a time

Web shop for social mode

Social casino monetisation is moving from app stores to own-brand web shops

Games Global networked progressives in NZ are for licensees only — an argument for a licensed brand

Responsible gambling tools that aren’t promoted are barely used — making them visible becomes a design task. The “one game at a time” rule rules out multi-window modes and autoplaying previews.

Conclusion

Clean execution beats novelty

Australian online hasn’t invented a taste of its own — it sells club-style Hold & Spin. The winner is whoever makes the coin, value, counter and jackpot ladder clearest.

New Zealand

The rules strip machines, jackpots, characters and luck from marketing. Competition moves inside the product: lobby tiles, in-game counters and the quality of responsible gambling screens.

Offshore brands moving to a licence will have to retrofit their interfaces. A product that designs session breaks, limits and honest win celebrations into its visual language from day one will enter a 15-brand market with a ready-made point of difference.
