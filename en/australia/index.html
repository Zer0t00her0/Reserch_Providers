01 — Key takeaway

Even online, Australia wants its Dragon Link

The market rests on one mechanic — Hold & Spin: valued coins lock in, the counter resets to three, and a linked Mini / Minor / Major / Grand jackpot sits above

Offshore operators have not invented anything new for Australians: they have recreated the club Dragon Link experience using suppliers that can operate offshore. Sound and light are amplifiers, not the main reason to play.

05 — Mechanic

How Hold & Spin works

01

Six bonus coins trigger three respins

02

Each new coin locks in and resets the counter to three

03

The round ends after three blank spins

04

A full grid awards the top progressive jackpot

Mini

Minor

Major

Grand

Why it works, according to suppliers: the mechanic is easy to grasp, players are “chasing the cash”, and gradually filling the grid makes the top prize feel visibly within reach.

Source: CDC Gaming (Light & Wonder, Gaming Arts, Bluberi). Supplier views, not player data

The mechanic is surrounded by a four-level linked jackpot, denomination choice and a bundle of several themed games on one link. Lightning Link adds a separate progressive jackpot to the link.

06 — What really has value

They copied the maths, not the dragons

$127.5m

paid by Light & Wonder to Aristocrat in January 2026, admitting use of Dragon Link maths in Dragon Train and permanently withdrawing the game

A 10-year lifespan

Lightning Link has lasted about 10 years, while almost none of the hundreds of games released each year last that long

Simple screen, complex model

Aristocrat’s product director credits its success to an intuitive layout on top of complex maths

Sources: InterGame, CDC Gaming (Matt Primmer, Aristocrat)

A designer’s job in this market is to make complex maths obvious on screen: the coin, the denomination, the counter, the jackpot ladder, the full grid.

In the AGRC survey, 17.4% of regular pokies players also played online pokies, even though domestic operators are banned from offering them. In New Zealand, 1.9% of adults play online pokies, rising to 3.8% among 15–24-year-olds.

12 — Graphics

Readability beats beauty

Coin value

large and instantly readable

Jackpot ladder

four levels with live amounts above the grid

Grid fill

the visible climax of the round

Signage

new Aristocrat cabinets invest in the stage around the screen

Red and gold is the standard

Dragons, gongs and coins across all leaders. The winner is not who breaks the standard, but who executes it more cleanly.

Same symbols in a family

Players were confused when the same symbols paid differently in similar games.

AI art and reskins

Studios complain of sameness; RubyPlay: AI does not understand how symbols relate.

Sources: CDC Gaming (Aristocrat), The Drop, Schottler 2014, CasinoBeats, Next.io. No direct AU/NZ research on graphic style

Aristocrat’s experience: the artist’s job is to make complex maths readable. Playson says players seek clarity and want to see a feature getting closer. Views on “sameness” are not backed by player surveys.

In Australia this is the subject of lawsuits and campaigns: the Dolphin Treasure case against Crown and Aristocrat (dismissed in 2018), and the “Losing Sound” project offering free loss sounds (2026). A UK rule since 2021 bans celebrating returns equal to or less than the stake.

14 — Characters

A franchise asset with local risk

Works

Aristocrat Hall of Fame

Lightning Link and Dragon Link (2025), Miss Kitty and Mo’ Mummy (2026). Miss Kitty is praised for how the character’s appearances build tension in free spins.

Risk

Stronger pull on at-risk players

Features with funny characters and role-play proved significantly more engaging for problem gamblers (Australia, 2014).

Advertising

Cartoon style is a target

UK ASA, 2025: Play’n GO banners with a rabbit, a robot DJ and anime princesses were ruled appealing to children.

Sources: casinos.com Hall of Fame, Schottler 2014, CMS / ASA. No data on specific Australian pokies characters

A character that explains the mechanic inside the game (collects coins, opens the grid) is safer than a cute mascot for banners. In New Zealand, online pokies are especially popular among 15–24-year-olds, so a cartoon character in advertising will be the regulator’s first target.

16 — Takeaways for slots

How to build a slot for AU/NZ

Hold & Win as the base

The counter reset to three is a big moment; each new coin lifts sound and light slightly

Jackpot ladder

Mini / Minor / Major / Grand with live amounts above the grid; in portrait, above the reels

Fortune theme, but cleaner

Dragons, gongs, coins or Egypt; stand out through clarity and the quality of coin animation

Celebration to scale

No celebration for a net loss, quiet for a small gain, loud for a big one

A slow-pace version

Victoria requires 3 seconds per spin; animation must stay engaging at a slow rhythm

A grown-up character

A character that collects coins and opens the grid, not a cute mascot for banners

A bundle of 4–6 games on one link: one maths model, several costumes, a shared jackpot pool

If you are building a game family, keep the same symbols and their values across all games — this is exactly where the Australian study recorded player confusion.

17 — Takeaways for the lobby

How to build a lobby for AU/NZ

A “Hold & Win / Link” row

A dedicated row by family (Coin Volcano, Dragon Pearls, Coin Strike) — players look for it themselves

Live jackpots on tiles

A badge with the current Grand and a shared linked-pool banner — the main reason to pick a machine

A calm winners feed

The equivalent of hearing others’ jackpots on the floor, but steady and without flashing

Limits in the NZ header

Licence badge, balance and session time — fast and unobtrusive, designed in from the start

A second showcase

Global high-volatility hits such as Gates of Olympus — in a separate row

Demand for Sunlight Princess outpaces its operator placement — the format is underrepresented

The NSW experience shows players reject cashless systems over privacy and inconvenience, so responsible gambling tools must be fast and unobtrusive.

Conclusion

The clearest, fairest hold-and-win

Not chasing the global multiplier trend, but making obvious on screen what players already love: the coin, the denomination, the counter and the jackpot ladder.

Window of opportunity

New Zealand: 15 brands for the whole country. Enter with proportionate celebration, visible limits and a calm winners feed from day one.

Competitors coming from offshore will have to rework their interfaces under regulatory pressure. Whoever builds in fair design from the start will gain an edge in a regulated market.
