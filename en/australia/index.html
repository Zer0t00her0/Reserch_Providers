02 — Glossary: gameplay

Slot terms used in this deck

Hold & Win

Bonus: valued coins stay on the grid, each new one resets the respin counter. Also called Hold & Spin, Link

Jackpot ladder

Fixed prizes Mini / Minor / Major / Grand. “2,000x” means 2,000 bets

RTP

Share of bets a slot returns to players on average. RTP 95.5%: of 100 wagered, ~95.5 comes back

Volatility

How unevenly a slot pays. High: wins are rarer but bigger

Max win

Maximum win, in bets: 10,345x is 10,345 bets

Bonus buy

Buying the bonus round outright at a fixed price, with no waiting

Cascades, pay anywhere

Winning symbols vanish and new ones drop from above; matching symbols pay anywhere on the grid, no lines. Example: Gates of Olympus

Loss disguised as a win (LDW)

A payout smaller than the bet, but with celebratory sound and animation

Definitions follow the report “Online slots in Australia and New Zealand”

Wild is a substitute symbol; scatter is a symbol that pays or triggers the bonus in any position. In Sunlight Princess three scatters award 8 free spins. Free spins are free rounds inside the bonus.

The Interactive Gambling Act bans offering online casinos to Australians. Since June 2024 credit cards and crypto are banned only at licensed bookmakers — offshore casinos accept them.

What it means: for a licensed product, Australia is a map of tastes, not a market to enter.

Sources: H2 Gambling Capital, Responsible Wagering Australia (via AGBrief), Blask, Dept of Infrastructure

The share of Australians’ crypto deposits is not published anywhere; there is no Stake data for Australia.

08 — Where the taste comes from

Online sells the club feel without club brands

Overlap with land-based casinos

Data on this slide is about machines in Australian clubs and pubs

Mechanics

Hold & Spin (Aristocrat’s name for Hold & Win) made club Lightning Link and Dragon Link among the highest-earning machines. Online leaders copy the mechanic and the Mini / Minor / Major / Grand ladder.

Themes

Egypt and Asian luck online echo club classics: Queen of the Nile and Dragon Link’s dragon themes. The link is inferred from titles and themes.

Club scale

~15,800 Dragon Link machines were installed in NSW and Queensland alone (May 2025). These originals aren’t available to Australians online for real money.

What it means: 3 Oaks and Playson probably lead because they bring the club format online. This is an inference, not a survey result: no studio names club pokies as a reference.

Sources: CDC Gaming (citing J.P. Morgan), PokiesAustralia (affiliate), SlotCatalog, OLBG

Online, the mechanic itself is nothing new: reviewers call Pragmatic’s Wolf Gold (2017), with its Money Respin on three resetting respins, widely copied. In 2022, 17.4% of regular club players also played online pokies. Affiliate PokiesAustralia explains Lightning Link’s appeal by “frequent small wins during Hold & Spin” — a medium-quality source.

Ads may not show physical gaming machines or play their sounds. What it means: the recognisability of club machines can live only inside the game, not in marketing.

16 — Visual language

Studios keep a familiar look on purpose

Playson, Coin Strike 2

Kept the original’s blue background, fruit, lighting and rock music. Added golden lightning, a new Super Strike symbol animation and layered sound; the grid grew from 3×3 to 3×4. Playson says sequels have sometimes outperformed originals, but gives no figures.

3 Oaks

Themes are chosen because players know them. Differentiation comes from modifiers: Extender adds three rows during respins — the grid change is visible at a glance.

A hit’s standard vocabulary: valued gold coins, lightning and fire, an object beside the grid (volcano, pot), a named jackpot ladder

17 — Animation and sound

Win sounds distort players’ memory

Reviews don’t break down the key Hold & Win animations — research exists only on sound

Win sound · experiment, Canada, 2013

+24%

overestimate with sound

+15%

without sound

96 people played two sessions on a slot simulator: with win sounds and without. Afterwards they estimated how many times they had won more than they bet. They overestimated the number of such wins in both sessions, but by 24% with sound and by 15% without.

What it means: with win sounds, players remember more wins than actually happened.

Online audit · UK, 2026

17 of 26

popular online slots

play a “winning” sound when the payout is smaller than the bet — a loss disguised as a win. Since 2021 a UK regulator rule (RTS 14F) bans celebrating a payout equal to or less than the bet.

What it means: celebration must match the size of the win — a regulatory requirement, not a matter of taste.

Sources: Dixon et al., University of Waterloo, 2013; Myles, Bennett, Newall, University of Bristol, 2026; UKGC RTS 14F

Waterloo (Dixon et al., 2013): visual feedback was present in both sessions; only the sound differed. With sound, players showed higher physiological arousal (skin conductance), and most preferred the session with sound. Bristol (2026): an audit of 26 popular UK online slots; 17 play a win-associated sound after a loss disguised as a win, 18 play sounds the authors judged celebratory; 400 surveyed players classified the sounds the same way as the authors. The authors blame vague rule wording that allows formal compliance. The same UK package banned spins faster than 2.5 seconds, autoplay and features that speed up play. In Australian venues, losses disguised as wins made up 18% of spins versus 14.2% for real wins (GRA, 2014).

Before the spin, while choosing a bet, the reels get ~54% of gaze and the balance ~14%.

Bangor University study, UK, 2017–2018: 118 betting shop customers played venue machines with their own money while wearing eye-tracking glasses.

The data comes from land-based machines, not online casinos. The study is exploratory and its recording counts are inconsistent, but the direction is clear.

What it means: anything the player must notice — coin value, win amount, the session notice required in NZ — should appear on the grid itself or right after the reels stop.

Source: Rogers et al., Bangor University, 2018 (funded by GambleAware)

Exact values during a spin: reels ~91.7%, balance ~5.1%, bet controls ~1% or less, gaze off the machine ~1.1%. Players with a high problem gambling score (PGSI 8+) looked at the win amount more often when choosing the next bet. Mobile legibility of coin values and the jackpot ladder in portrait has not been tested by anyone; 3×3 and 3×4 grids logically fit a vertical screen better than 6×5 — an inference from dimensions, not a test. Nolimit City estimates its mobile share at about 75%.

19 — Characters

Two ways to build a franchise

Mascot

Elvis Frog, Zeus

BGaming designer: “art attracts players, maths keeps them”. Elvis was designed to be bright and instantly recognisable. Zeus in Gates of Olympus launches orbs that multiply wins.

Object

3 Oaks and Playson coins

The brand rests on a coin, volcano, pot, lightning. The princess in Sunlight Princess is just a wild (substitute symbol); the press release doesn’t mention her at all.

Risk

Character in ads

In NZ a fictional character can’t be the face of advertising. In the UK in 2025 the advertising regulator ruled against banners with cartoon characters — children had seen them.

Overlap with land-based casinos

Data from Australian venues

In Australian venues (2014), features with funny characters were more engaging for problem gamblers. Aristocrat’s club Miss Kitty and Mo’ Mummy entered the casinos.com 2026 hall of fame — a vote, not a survey.

Sources: European Gaming (BGaming), Gambling.com NZ, CMS / ASA, Schottler 2014, casinos.com

Play'n GO banners featuring a superhero Easter bunny, a cartoon robot DJ and anime-style princesses were shown next to email inboxes accessible to children (ASA, July 2025). The 3 Oaks coin strategy, where the brand rests on an object rather than a character, is safer from a regulatory standpoint in New Zealand. In-game themes are not restricted — the risk lies in marketing use of the character.

20 — Takeaways for online slots

How to build an online slot for AU/NZ

Hold & Win as the base

A coin with a large, instantly readable value is the main asset; the respin counter reset is the main event

Ladder above the grid

Mini / Minor / Major / Grand always visible; values are the highest-contrast elements, since gaze is on the grid during a spin

Familiar costume, cleaner

Egypt, Asian luck, fruit; stand out through coin animation and symbol clarity, not by leaving the theme

Celebrate by size

Don’t celebrate a payout equal to or less than the bet (required in the UK); a small gain is quiet, a big one is loud

No autoplay or turbo

Autoplay is banned in NZ and quick spin is in question — the game must hold attention at a manual pace. Bonus buy for NZ should be switchable

Character inside the mechanic

The character triggers or explains the mechanic rather than fronting ads — that is banned in NZ

Sequels on the Playson model: keep the look, boost one effect, enlarge the grid slightly

Visible modifiers like 3 Oaks’ Extender provide a reason for new animation that is rare in this genre. Reviewers don’t break down precisely the key Hold & Win animations — that is room for differentiation. Keep bonus buy for NZ configurable until the DIA regulator issues technical standards.

21 — Takeaways for the lobby

How to build an online lobby for AU/NZ

“Hold & Win / Link” row

By family: Coin Volcano, Dragon Pearls, Coin Strike, Sun of Egypt. Sunlight Princess is searched more than it’s listed — players seek out the format themselves

Second shelf

Global high-volatility hits (Gates of Olympus, Sweet Bonanza) in a separate row: they have a different audience

Jackpots inside the lobby

In NZ slot jackpots can’t be advertised — a live Grand badge on the tile and a shared jackpot banner become the main showcase

Responsible gambling as UI

Session timer in the header, a pause screen with countdown, limits in onboarding, one game at a time — these are mandatory screens in NZ

Web shop for social mode

Aristocrat’s social casino already sells 24% via its own web shop vs 13% a year earlier — monetisation is leaving the app stores

Games Global network jackpots in NZ are available only to licensees — an argument for a licensed brand

Responsible gambling tools that aren’t promoted are barely used — making them visible becomes a design task. The “one game at a time” rule rules out multi-window modes and autoplaying previews. Social games with simulated gambling are rated R18+ in Australia, so an age gate is needed in social mode too.

Conclusion

Clean execution beats novelty

Australian online hasn’t invented a taste of its own — it sells club Hold & Spin. The winner is whoever has the clearest coin, value, counter and jackpot ladder.

New Zealand

The rules strip machines, jackpots, characters and luck out of marketing. Competition moves inside the product: lobby tiles, in-game counters and the quality of responsible gambling screens.

Offshore brands moving to a licence will have to retrofit their interfaces. A product that designs session breaks, limits and honest celebration into its visual language from day one will enter a 15-brand market with a ready-made edge.
